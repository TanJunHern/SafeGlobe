# Third-Party Due Diligence and Sanctions Compliance Policy

**Meridian Commodities Pte Ltd**
**Policy owner:** Chief Compliance Officer
**Version:** 3.2 (mock, for testing)
**Effective:** 1 October 2026

## Section 1: Purpose and scope

Clause 1.1: This policy sets the minimum checks Meridian Commodities ("the Company") carries out before and during any business relationship with a third party.

Clause 1.2: It applies to every organisation, individual and vessel the Company buys from, sells to, charters, appoints or invests in. These are called counterparties.

Clause 1.3: No contract may be signed, and no payment made, until the counterparty has a request status of Approved.

Clause 1.4: Every employee who proposes a counterparty must submit an onboarding request with accurate details and supporting documents.

## Section 2: Contract value thresholds

Clause 2.1: A counterparty with an anticipated contract value above SGD 100,000 must complete a Due Diligence Questionnaire (DDQ) and have its beneficial ownership verified.

Clause 2.2: A contract above SGD 500,000 also needs sign-off by a senior compliance officer and review of audited financial statements.

Clause 2.3: Contracts must not be split to stay under a threshold. Related contracts with the same counterparty in a 12-month period are added together.

## Section 3: Politically exposed persons and government links

Clause 3.1: A Politically Exposed Person (PEP) is an individual who holds or has held a prominent public function in the last 5 years, and their family members and close associates.

Clause 3.6: A counterparty is subject to Enhanced Due Diligence where the individual, or any director, senior executive or beneficial owner holding more than 10%, is a PEP. A DDQ and a source-of-wealth check are mandatory.

Clause 3.7: A counterparty in which a government or state-owned enterprise holds 10% or more is treated in the same way as a PEP-linked counterparty.

Clause 3.8: Success fees, and payments to an account in a country other than where the counterparty operates or is registered, need written approval from the Chief Compliance Officer.

## Section 4: Country and jurisdiction risk

Clause 4.1: A counterparty that is incorporated in, resident in, operating from or flagged in a country scoring below 40 on the Transparency International Corruption Perceptions Index must complete a DDQ.

Clause 4.2: The following are high-risk jurisdictions whatever their score: Russia, Iran, North Korea, Syria, Myanmar, Cuba, Venezuela, Belarus, Yemen, Libya, Sudan and Somalia.

Clause 4.3: A counterparty in a jurisdiction under comprehensive sanctions may be approved only by the Chief Compliance Officer after legal advice.

## Section 5: Entity-specific controls

Clause 5.1 (Organisations): Registration number, directors and beneficial owners holding 25% or more must be recorded and checked against a registry extract issued in the last 3 months.

Clause 5.2 (Intermediaries): Agents, brokers, consultants and other representatives who act for the Company must complete a DDQ whatever the contract value.

Clause 5.3 (Individuals): Full name, date of birth, nationality and identity document number must be recorded and checked against the document.

Clause 5.4 (Vessels): A vessel must complete a vessel DDQ where it flies a flag of convenience (including Panama, Liberia and the Marshall Islands), where its AIS record shows a gap of more than 12 hours, or where it has carried out ship-to-ship transfers that cannot be explained.

Clause 5.5 (Vessel identity): Every vessel must be identified by its IMO number. The number is checked on VesselFinder (https://www.vesselfinder.com) before the request is accepted. A request with an IMO number that does not exist is refused.

Clause 5.6 (Registry mismatch): Where the name, flag or type recorded for the IMO number differs from what was declared, the difference is recorded as a finding, the request is treated as at least medium risk, and it cannot be approved without compliance review.

## Section 6: Screening and decisions

Clause 6.1: Every counterparty is screened against sanctions lists (UN, OFAC, EU, UK and MAS), PEP data, adverse media and the Company's own restricted-party list.

Clause 6.2: A confirmed sanctions match is rejected. Only the Chief Compliance Officer may reverse a rejection.

Clause 6.3: Outcomes are:

| Outcome | When it applies | Who decides |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Approved (green) | No policy clause is triggered and screening is clear | Automatic |
| Pending DDQ (orange) | A clause in Sections 2 to 5 is triggered | Compliance officer, after the DDQ |
| Escalate or reject (red) | Sanctions match, PEP with unresolved concerns, or sanctions or corruption history disclosed in the DDQ | Senior compliance officer |

Clause 6.4: A DDQ with unclear, incomplete or inconsistent answers is returned to the counterparty for correction. It may be returned more than once.

Clause 6.5: A DDQ that discloses a sanctions breach, a corruption or money laundering matter, or a government or PEP connection is escalated. It is not returned for correction.

## Section 7: Ongoing monitoring

Clause 7.1: An approved counterparty with ongoing monitoring enabled is re-screened at the frequency set by Compliance: daily, weekly, monthly or yearly.

Clause 7.2: A monitored vessel has its position and destination refreshed from VesselFinder at every re-screen. A change of destination raises an alert.

Clause 7.3: A change in screening result reopens the request for compliance review.

## Section 8: Records and breaches

Clause 8.1: Onboarding requests, DDQ answers, screening results and decisions are kept for at least 5 years after the relationship ends.

Clause 8.2: An employee who gives false information in a request, or who deals with a counterparty that is not Approved, is subject to disciplinary action.

Clause 8.3: Concerns may be reported in confidence to compliance@meridian-commodities.example.

*Mock policy for testing. Meridian Commodities is a fictional company.*
