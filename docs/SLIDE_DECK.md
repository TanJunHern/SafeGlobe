# DueDilly showcase deck: slide-by-slide outline

Written 11 Oct 2026. Screens and URLs were checked against the app running on `http://localhost:8080`.

**Intent of the deck:** show a non-specialist audience that checking a new business partner is slow because three groups of people pass paperwork back and forth, and that DueDilly puts all three on one flow: the employee who asks, the counterparty who answers, and the compliance officer who decides.

**Story in one line:** Problem (it takes months) → why (three parties, manual hand-offs) → demo (one request followed through all three screens) → what happens after approval → how it is built → recap.

## 2. Before taking screenshots

1. Start the app: `npm run dev`, then open `http://localhost:8080/login`.
2. Optional but recommended: `npm run demo:reset` returns the data to the scripted demo state. It replaces the current demo data (a backup is written to `demo-data/backups/`), so only run it if nothing in the current data needs keeping. The live data today has drifted from the script: for example Northwind Bunkering shows "Pending DDQ" instead of "DDQ Under Review".
3. Counterparty links contain a one-time token. The full links are in `demo-data/DEMO-LINKS.md` (regenerated by the reset). The pattern is `http://localhost:8080/ddq/portal?token=<JWT>`.
4. Personas are switched from the login page, or from the user badge at the top right. No password is needed in the demo.
5. The compliance app is one page. Globe, Reviews, Compliance, DDQ, Sources and Policies are tabs in the left rail and have no address of their own: open the base URL, then click the tab.
6. The Compliance Agent panel showed **"Placeholder mode (no model)"** during this check, which means Gemini did not answer. Fix the key or quota and press "Re-run" before capturing slides 13 and 14, otherwise the screenshot contradicts the AI story.

### Slide 2: Overview

- **Elements:** three numbered blocks, left to right

### Slide 3: Three terms, in plain words

- **Title:** Three terms you need
- **Subheading:** No compliance background required
- **Elements:** three cards
  - **KYC (Know Your Counterparty):** checking that a company, person or ship is who they say they are, and is not banned or linked to crime. Like a background check before you sign a contract.
  - **DDQ (Due Diligence Questionnaire):** a form the partner fills in about themselves: who owns them, any court cases, any political links. Like a detailed job application, for a company.
  - **Risk intelligence:** keeping watch after approval. Sanctions lists, ownership and country risk change, so yesterday's safe partner can become today's problem.
  - Small footnote glossary: *Sanctions* = government ban lists. *PEP* = politically exposed person, such as a minister or their family. *UBO* = the real human owner behind a company.
- **Screenshot:** none. Use one icon per card.

- **Subheading:** The delay is not only a compliance problem

### Slide 6: Hook, what the hold-up is

- **Title:** The hold-up is the hand-offs
- **Subheading:** Three parties, three inboxes, no shared view
- **Elements:**
  - Diagram: Employee → email → Compliance → email + PDF → Counterparty → email → Compliance → email → Employee. Mark each arrow with a clock icon.
  - Four pain points beside it:
    1. **Retyping:** analysts spend nearly half their time turning raw data into a usable format (Arachnys survey of 156 AML/KYC professionals, 2020)
    2. **Silos:** 86% of banks blame poor data management and siloed processes for losing clients (Fenergo)
    3. **Long forms:** a 100-question DDQ takes a supplier 4 to 5 hours for a first draft, before any review (vendor estimate, Wolfia)
    4. **Spreadsheets and chasing:** 57% name poor vendor communication or spreadsheet-based assessments among their biggest third-party risk problems (Prevalent study)
- **Screenshot:** none.
- **Speaker note:** points 3 and 4 come from vendors, not independent surveys. Present them as estimates.

### Slide 8: The lifecycle

- **Title:** The lifecycle of one request
- **Subheading:** Four steps, two possible paths
- **Elements:** flow diagram

  2. **Screen** (AI): sanctions, political links and company policy, in seconds

### Slide 25: Tech stack, how it is built

- **Title:** Under the hood
- **Subheading:** Google AI and Google Cloud, with a fallback at every step
- **Elements:** layered diagram, top to bottom
  - **Three web portals** (plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript): Employee, Counterparty, Compliance. Google Maps Places for pinning addresses.
  - **Identity:** Sign in with Google (Google Identity Services) for staff; a signed one-time link (JWT) for the counterparty; role-based access for employee and compliance officer.
  - **Application:** Node.js with Express 5, one container. REST API (`/api/kyc`, `/api/ddq`, `/api/monitoring`, `/v1/screen`). Excel export with ExcelJS.
  - **AI:** Gemini through Google AI Studio. One shared client with a three-model fallback chain (`gemini-flash-lite-latest` → `gemini-flash-latest` → `gemini-3.1-flash-lite`) and a no-model mode, so the app keeps working when a model is busy or offline.
  - **Compliance Agent:** an agent with seven tools, built in the same shape as Google's Agent Development Kit (ADK) so the runtime can be swapped in.
  - **Data:** SQLite today; uploaded documents on local disk or Cloud Storage.
  - **Hosting:** Docker image on Google Cloud Run, region `asia-southeast1` (Singapore). Secrets in Secret Manager. Cloud Build for deployment.
- **Screenshot:** none. Optional: `http://localhost:8080/healthz` output as a small code inset.
