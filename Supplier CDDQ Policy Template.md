# Supplier CDDQ Policy Template

Oct 9, 2026 · @h

## Purpose and instructions

&#91;Issuing Company\] requires every new supplier to complete this Counterparty Due Diligence Questionnaire (CDDQ) before onboarding, and to update it every \[24\] months or when its circumstances change. The answers let us assess bribery, corruption, conflict of interest and sanctions risk.

**How to complete it**

1. Answer every question. Write "Not applicable" with a reason rather than leaving a field blank.
2. Where a question asks Yes / No and you answer Yes, give full details in the Response column or on a separate sheet referenced by question number.
3. Answers cover the Supplier, its parent company, subsidiaries and any entity it controls, unless a question says otherwise.
4. Capitalised terms (for example Government Official, Beneficial Owner) have the meanings in the Appendix.
5. Return the completed and signed questionnaire, with the supporting documents below, to \[compliance@issuingcompany.com\] within \[14\] days.

**Supporting documents to attach**

- [ ] Certificate of incorporation or business registration extract (issued within the last 3 months)
- [ ] Shareholder register and group ownership chart down to the Beneficial Owners
- [ ] Identity document for each Beneficial Owner and each director
- [ ] Copies of the policies referred to in Section 6
- [ ] Latest audited financial statements

## Appendix: Definitions

These terms apply wherever they appear capitalised in this questionnaire.

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Adverse Media | Negative news or public reports linking a person or company to financial crime, corruption, Sanctions breaches, serious misconduct or reputational risk. |
| Beneficial Owner | An individual who ultimately owns or controls 25% or more of the shares or voting rights of the Supplier, directly or through other entities, or who otherwise exercises control over its management or decisions. |
| Bribery / Corruption | Offering, giving, requesting or accepting anything of value, directly or through others, to improperly influence a person in their role or to obtain a business advantage. |
| Close Associate | An individual with a known close business relationship with a person, including joint ownership of an entity or legal arrangement, or sole ownership of an entity set up for that person's benefit. |
| Conflict of Interest | A situation where a personal, family, financial or other interest could influence, or appear to influence, a person's objective judgement in dealings between the Supplier and \[Issuing Company\]. |
| Dual-Use Goods | Goods, software and technology that can be used for both civilian and military purposes, as listed under applicable export control regimes. |
| Facilitation Payment | A small, unofficial payment to a Government Official to speed up or secure a routine action they are already obliged to perform, such as issuing a permit or clearing customs. |
| Family Member | A person's spouse or partner, parents, children and their spouses or partners, siblings, and any other relative living in the same household. |
| Government Entity | Any national, regional or local government, ministry, department, agency, court, legislature, public international organisation, or a State-Owned Enterprise. |
| Government Official | Any officer or employee of a Government Entity; anyone acting in an official capacity for one; any political party, party official or candidate; and any member of a royal family with official duties. |
| Intermediary | A third party, such as an agent, consultant, broker, distributor or freight forwarder, who acts on behalf of the Supplier or interacts with others for it. |
| Money Laundering | Concealing or disguising the origin of proceeds of crime so they appear legitimate, or dealing with property known or suspected to be such proceeds. |
| Politically Exposed Person (PEP) | An individual who holds, or has held in the last \[5\] years, a prominent public function in any country, such as a head of state or government, minister, senior politician, senior judge, senior military officer, senior executive of a State-Owned Enterprise, or a senior official of an international organisation. |
| Sanctioned Jurisdiction | A country or territory subject to comprehensive, country-wide Sanctions. \[Compliance to insert the current list maintained by Issuing Company.\] |
| Sanctioned Person | Any individual, entity or vessel named on a Sanctions list, or owned 50% or more, or controlled, by one or more such persons. |
| Sanctions | Economic, financial or trade restrictions imposed by a Sanctions Authority, including asset freezes, trade embargoes and export or import restrictions. |
| Sanctions Authority | The United Nations Security Council, the Monetary Authority of Singapore, the United States (including OFAC and the Department of State), the European Union, the United Kingdom, and any other authority with jurisdiction over the Supplier or \[Issuing Company\]. |
| Senior Management | Directors, the chief executive, and any officer with authority over the Supplier's management, finance or compliance. |
| State-Owned Enterprise | A company or body that is owned 50% or more, or controlled, by a Government Entity. |
